A thesis built to make hyperspectral analysis more usable

On 20 February I defended my IUSS diploma thesis: “Web-Enabled and User-Friendly Python Pipeline for Rare Earth Elements Mapping from Hyperspectral Imagery.”

The project brought together remote sensing, hyperspectral data analysis and software development. The aim was to make a complex processing workflow more approachable and usable through a web-enabled Python pipeline for mapping rare earth elements.

From research method to useful tool

The thesis grew out of an intense research experience with the IUSS Pavia Taramelli Group. I am grateful to Professor Andrea Taramelli and Dr Sara Liburdi for their guidance, support and encouragement throughout the work.

That thesis now connects to a broader research direction: combining the structure of spectral physics with machine learning to make detection methods more reliable and easier to inspect. At the same time, I returned to Almo Collegio Borromeo for the Feast of Saint Charles and received the College diploma, closing a formative chapter in Pavia.
