Honorable Mention at the 4th EELISA Scientific Student Competition

At the 4th EELISA Scientific Student Competition in Madrid, I presented a hybrid approach for reliable rare-earth-element detection in hyperspectral imagery and received an Honorable Mention.

The work, “From Spectral Physics to AI: A Hybrid Approach for Reliable Rare Earth Element Detection in Hyperspectral Imagery,” asks how physical knowledge about spectral signatures can help machine learning produce more robust and interpretable results.

Presentation and discussion

Presenting alongside students and researchers from across the EELISA European University alliance gave me the opportunity to exchange ideas, receive feedback and discuss how physics-informed AI can serve scientific applications. The recognition encourages me to keep exploring the intersection of physics, systems theory and AI, with a focus on reliable and trustworthy models.

This presentation builds on my broader work in hyperspectral remote sensing and the Python pipeline developed for my IUSS diploma thesis.
