01 / Medical robotics · Main research focus

Robotics for
gentler interventions.

Three projects, one question: how compact, controllable robotic systems can work safely inside the body — from magnetic actuation at Sant’Anna to MRI-compatible devices at the Max Planck Institute and autonomous endovascular navigation.

01 · Sant’Anna · 2026–present

Magnetic actuation on an endoscopic device.

The challenge. Endoscopic devices need actuation that is compact enough to sit on the device itself, while still allowing it to be steered and controlled precisely.

My work. As an intern research engineer in Medical Robotics at Sant’Anna School of Advanced Studies, I study local magnetic control strategies using miniaturized coils mounted on an endoscopic device. The project is ongoing: methods and results will be added here as they become shareable.

Supervisor: Prof. V. Iacovacci · Pisa, Italy

02 · Max Planck Institute · Jun–Sep 2025

MRI-compatible and soft medical systems.

The challenge. Treatment and intervention under MRI guidance bring together mechanics, materials, control and imaging constraints: devices must be actuated and controlled while remaining compatible with the scanner.

My work. As a Robotics Institute Germany (RIGI) intern in the Medical Systems group of the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems, I worked on coils for robotically assisted, on-demand hyperthermia for tissue ablation, and on a hydraulically actuated soft catheter for MRI-guided interventions.

Supervisor: Dr. sc. Jelena Zinnanti · Stuttgart, Germany

03 · Siemens Healthineers · October 2026

Autonomous endovascular navigation.

The challenge. Make guidewires and catheters navigate autonomously during endovascular procedures, using only what can be observed at deployment: fluoroscopy images.

Our approach. With team π-SA we built a parallelized physics simulator to generate demonstrations, trained privileged reinforcement-learning experts with Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), and distilled them into SmolVLA (450M), a vision-language-action policy that steers from fluoroscopy images and text instructions.

Result. First prize in the endovascular robotics challenge and in the overall Siemens Healthineers Robotics & AI Hackathon.

Team: Giuseppe Cairone, Alessio Naldoni, Riccardo Panizzolo and Alberto Anelli · Erlangen, Germany
